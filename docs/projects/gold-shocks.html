Causal evidence from the Peruvian Amazon (2008-2023)

We estimate the causal effect of international gold price fluctuations on local social conflicts in Madre de Dios, using a Shift-Share IV approach and satellite imagery data.

1. Overview

Does the boom in extractive industries fuel social unrest? Focusing on the Peruvian Amazon, we exploit exogenous variation in international gold prices combined with local mineral endowments to predict conflict probability at the district level.

We leverage data from three main sources: 1. Ombudsman’s Office (Defensoría del Pueblo): Monthly panel of social conflicts. 2. Sentinel-2 Satellite Imagery: To measure illegal mining deforestation. 3. MEF (SIAF): For local canon transfers analysis.

2. Methodology

We employ a Shift-Share Instrumental Variable (SSIV) strategy. The logic is to isolate the component of local economic growth driven purely by external price shocks.

\[ Z_{dt} = \text{Endowment}_d \times \log(\text{Price}_{t}) \]

Where \(Z_{dt}\) is the instrument for district \(d\) at time \(t\).

3. Implementation Code

Here is how we construct the instrument and run the core specification. Notice how we handle the spatial lag.

  • Stata (Estimation)
  • Python (Spatial Viz)
* 1. Define the Shift-Share Instrument
gen shift_share_iv = gold_endowment * log(gold_price_int)

* 2. Main Specification (Fixed Effects)
reghdfe conflict_dummy shift_share_iv canon_transfers ///
    population_density, ///
    absorb(district_id year_month) ///
    vce(cluster district_id)

* 3. Export Results
outreg2 using "results/main_table.tex", replace tex
import geopandas as gpd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Load District Data with Conflict Intensity
peru_map = gpd.read_file("data/peru_districts.geojson")

# Plot
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 1, figsize=(10, 10))
peru_map.plot(column='conflict_intensity', 
              ax=ax, 
              legend=True,
              cmap='OrRd',
              scheme='quantiles')

plt.title("Spatial Distribution of Social Conflicts (2023)")
plt.show()

4. Preliminary Results

The results suggest that a 10% increase in gold prices leads to a 2.5 percentage point increase in the probability of a socio-environmental conflict emerging in the subsequent quarter.

“The effect is driven primarily by competition for land rights rather than direct labor disputes.”
